Who does what

The module splits in two, and knowing which half you are talking to removes most of the confusion.

The Blocks Utilities portal authors the catalogue. An admin builds plans, meters, entitlements, trials and prices there. Your application never creates a plan.

Your application sells and enforces. It subscribes an organization, asks what that organization may do, and records what it used. Those are the only calls you need.

Organization scope

Every subscription belongs to an organization within a tenant. Your token carries its own organization and that always wins — an organizationId in a request body is silently ignored unless the caller is the platform console. If you are integrating an application, omit it entirely; you are already scoped.

The model

A plan is a menu item. Everything below hangs off it, and each part answers a different question.

The distinction that catches people

A meter counts; an entitlement decides. They are separate records and nothing forces them to agree. A plan permitting 500 signatures against a meter including 150 will tell your app all 500 are allowed and bill 350 of them as overage. Keep the two numbers identical unless you specifically want that.

Tax

Tax is configured per price, and it is optional. A price states a rate in basis points and which of two things that rate means: exclusive, where the tax is added to the configured amount, or inclusive, where the configured amount is what the customer pays and the tax is found inside it. The same "CHF 145.00 at 7.7%" is CHF 156.17 to a customer under the first and CHF 145.00 under the second.

This is manually configured tax, not tax determination. There is no jurisdiction detection, no address or VAT-number resolution, no Stripe Tax, and no tax registration or reporting. What a price says is what is charged, wherever the customer is. Deciding the correct rate for a customer is the merchant's, not this module's.

One rate applies to everything that price charges for: the first payment, every renewal, quantity changes and their proration, plan changes in both directions, and metered overage. The order is always the same — price → discount → tax → credit:

  • Discounts come first, so tax is charged on what the customer actually pays rather than on a list price nobody paid.
  • Tax is calculated once, on the aggregate. Not per seat, not per meter: taxing lines separately and summing them produces a total that the lines cannot explain.
  • Credit comes last. Banked credit pays a bill; it does not change what the bill was for, so it never reduces the tax.
  • Explicit tax modes round to the nearest minor unit, halves up. 7.7% of CHF 145.00 is CHF 11.17. Legacy subscriptions without a stored mode retain the former truncation rule.

A subscription is charged from the price's tax as snapshotted when it was sold. Editing a catalogue price's tax never reprices an existing subscriber, exactly as with the amount and the interval. A plan change settles each side at its own snapshot's rate and mode before the two are netted, so moving between an inclusive and an exclusive price prorates correctly.

Prices authored before tax modes existed carry a rate and no mode. Those are exclusive — that is how they have always been charged — and the API reports them as such rather than leaving a client to guess.

Invoices. Every invoice shows a subtotal, each discount source on its own line, and a tax line naming the rate and whether it was added or already inside the price. When banked subscription credit is consumed it also shows a negative credit line below tax, so the lines add up to exactly what was charged.

The first payment now produces an invoice too. It used to not: signup is a hosted checkout session and the provider composed no invoice for it, so invoice history began at the first renewal. Documents are now issued by this application rather than read back from the provider, so the initial charge, every trial start and every refund all have one. A client may promise a signup invoice; it appears within seconds of the payment confirming, not synchronously with it.

Automatic price discounts

A price can take a percentage off by itself, with nothing typed by the subscriber. This is how a plan sells "8% off if you pay yearly": the yearly price carries automaticDiscountBasisPoints, the monthly price beside it under the same plan carries none, and both remain prices on one plan rather than two products.

It lives on the price rather than the plan because the offer is cadence-specific, and because a plan sold in two currencies has two prices that may not be discounted alike. A missing or zero value is no automatic discount, which is what every price authored before this existed carries.

A subscriber can therefore hold two reductions the merchant authored — the price's own discount and the volume band their quantity selects — plus one they redeemed. The two authored ones are settled first, by the price:

  • BestDiscount applies whichever of the two takes off more money, and only that one. The default, and the value a price with no stated combination is calculated as.
  • Additive adds the two rates and applies the sum once to the gross. 8% for the cadence plus 5% for the volume is 13% off, not the 12.6% that applying one after the other would give. The sum is capped at 100%.

The result is the built-in discount. The plan's quantityDiscountCombinationPolicy then decides what a promotional code adds to it, exactly as it already decided between a band and a code: BestDiscount takes whichever of the built-in result and the promotion saves more, Stack reduces what the built-in discount left, and QuantityOnly ignores the code — that value now means "built-in discounts only", and its stored wire value is unchanged.

The full order of operations, which every money path shares:

  • Gross — the price's amount times the quantity it charges for.
  • Built-in discount — automatic and band, combined as the price says. Truncated to the minor unit, matching the volume bands that already existed: a reduction of 5% on 199 takes off 9 rather than 10, so truncation leans very slightly toward the merchant.
  • Promotional code — combined as the plan says.
  • Tax — once, on the aggregate, on what is left. See Tax.
  • Credit — banked credit pays the bill last, and never reduces the tax.

A promotion that loses is not consumed, and that now includes losing to an automatic discount: three months of "2% off" are not spent on periods where the price's own 8% was larger.

The automatic discount is snapshotted when the subscription is sold, like the amount, the interval and the tax. Clearing it from the catalogue tomorrow does not raise anybody's renewal, and adding one does not lower anybody's. A plan change snapshots the target price's discount and prorates both sides at their own.

Metered overage is charged by the price the subscription was sold on, so the price's automatic discount applies to an overage invoice too, before its tax. A volume band does not — it prices units of a quantity item, and a meter has none — and a promotional code does not reach usage invoices at all.

Invoices. A subscription response, a quantity preview and an overage invoice each report what came off: the automatic rate, the band's rate, the combination, the gross, the built-in reduction, the promotional reduction and the amount that was then taxed. GET /api/subscriptions/invoices carries the same breakdown per issued document in its amounts object, recorded when the charge was raised rather than recomputed from a catalogue that has since moved — and there the automatic rate and the band's rate appear as two separate amounts rather than one combined reduction. A plan-change or quantity settlement also reports settlement, because its amount is a subtraction rather than a discounted price: 

"settlement": {
  "outgoing": {                  // the period being left, part-way through
    "grossAmountMinor": 1000, "builtInDiscountMinor": 100,
    "promotionalDiscountMinor": 50, "discountedAmountMinor": 850,
    "taxAmountMinor": 85, "periodTotalMinor": 935,
    "proratedValueMinor": 467    // unused value credited back
  },
  "target": {                    // the period being joined
    "grossAmountMinor": 2000, "builtInDiscountMinor": 160,
    "promotionalDiscountMinor": 0, "discountedAmountMinor": 1840,
    "taxAmountMinor": 184, "periodTotalMinor": 2024,
    "proratedValueMinor": 1012   // remaining time charged for
  },
  "creditConsumedMinor": 100,
  "netSettlementMinor": 445      // target prorated − outgoing unused − credit
}

Each side is priced as its own period, with its own discounts and its own tax rate and mode — a change can cross between an inclusive and an exclusive price — and only then prorated. netSettlementMinor is negative where a downgrade banked credit rather than charging, which is why it is not simply the amount charged. The flat renewal fields are absent on these rows, and settlement is absent on renewal rows: they describe the same charge in the only shape that fits it.

settlement is absent on a renewal, an overage and an initial charge, whose amounts describe them in full. Both the two rates and the settlement are absent from payments raised before either was recorded; an initial charge is reconstructed from the terms it was sold on, since every input to it is snapshotted on the subscription, and is reported as a single gross figure only where that reconstruction does not reconcile to the amount actually taken.

Provider invoice lines continue to show the discounted subtotal rather than a separate discount line — the lines have to add up to what was charged, and a gross line plus a reduction line is a different invoice shape than the one this module writes.

Authoring bands, and what they are not

Bands belong to a quantity item, not to the plan. One user item with five bands covers every seat count — a plan per band would be five plans a subscriber has to be moved between, and moving between them is a plan change with its own proration. Changing quantity inside one plan simply selects the band the new quantity falls in.

The band is chosen by the total quantity, and its discount applies to the whole charge. Ten users in a 10% band is ten users at 10% off, not four at full price and six discounted. This is volume pricing; graduated pricing, where each band prices only the units inside it, is not what this does.

Volume bands and discount codes are separate mechanisms. A band comes from the quantity held; a code is redeemed by the subscriber. When both apply, the plan's quantityDiscountCombinationPolicy decides which wins or whether they stack — see Buying more, or fewer, units.

The plan builder writes all of this: enable Apply volume discounts on a quantity item and edit the bands as a table of from, to and percent. Percentages there are basis points here — 5% is 500. The combination policy is authored alongside them, and is sent on every write: a plan updated without it is reset to BestDiscount, which silently changes what its subscribers are charged.

Reset policy belongs to each meter. A plan can therefore include one million tokens with Periodic reset and 5 GB storage with Never reset. If the subscriber has consumed 3 GB when renewal runs, the storage balance remains 3 GB and only 2 GB is available; renewal does not grant another 5 GB. Existing meters default to Periodic. Record uploads as positive byte quantities and deletions as negative quantities. Negative usage is accepted only for Never meters and cannot reduce their balance below zero.

Carrying an unused allowance forward

A CarryForward meter is billed, rated and reported exactly like a Periodic one. The only difference is the allowance each window opens with: the plan's includedQuantity plus whatever the window before it did not use, bounded by carryForwardCap. The roll compounds one window at a time, and the cap bounds every hop.

Five cases carry nothing, each deliberately. A window inside a trial, because a trial grant is meant to be the whole trial allowance and not a float to bank. A window that overlapped the trial, so nothing rolls out of one either. The first window of a subscription, which has no predecessor. Any window that preceded a plan change, since a change re-anchors the usage schedule and carrying across it would make repeated changes a way to collect allowance. And a window that went into overage, which carries zero rather than a negative — that overage was already invoiced, and letting it reduce the next allowance would charge for it twice.

A window in which nothing was recorded has no counter at all, and carries the plan's includedQuantity as fully unused. It does not pass on anything it had itself carried in, which is not recoverable without a document — so a long idle stretch rolls one window's worth, not the whole accumulation.

GET /api/entitlements reports the carried allowance as the entitlement's limit for these meters, rather than the entitlement's own declared number. The two would otherwise disagree, and a caller would be told it had run out while POST /api/subscription-usage still permitted the action.

Trial

trialDays sets the length. A trial grant replaces a meter's allowance rather than adding to it — a 5-signature grant against a 150 meter means 5 during the trial. A meter with no grant keeps its whole monthly allowance for the trial's duration, which on anything costly is an invitation to sign up, consume and leave.

Billing profile

Who an organization's invoices are addressed to: a legal name, a billing contact, and optionally an address and a tax registration number. One per organization, filled in before the first paid subscription and reused by every one after it.

Distinct from the billing account, which is the organization's standing with one payment provider — a customer id, a saved card. An organization can hold several of those and they must all print the same name.

A complete profile is required before money moves. Starting a paid subscription, changing plan, or adding units is refused with subscription_billing_profile_incomplete while the legal name, contact name or contact email is missing, and the response names the missing fields. Free plans, previews, quantity decreases and renewals are never blocked: none of them is a moment a person can be asked to fill a form in, and a renewal that refused to charge over a form would cost the subscriber their service.

The address and the tax id are deliberately not required. A great many subscribers are individuals with neither, and refusing them a subscription over a field their jurisdiction does not ask for would be a billing rule invented here.

Every value is copied onto each document as it is issued. Editing the profile changes what future documents say and never what an issued one says — so a subscriber who renames their company keeps last year's invoices under the old name, which is what an auditor expects.

Merchant profile

Who is selling. One per tenant: a legal name, and optionally a trading name, an address, a tax registration, a support address and payment instructions. The counterpart of the billing profile — that one says who is buying.

Tenant-scoped, and console-writable only. An invoice names a seller in law, so a subscriber able to set this could have their own invoices issued under a company of their choosing. PUT is refused for everyone but the platform console with subscription_merchant_profile_forbidden. GET is open to any authenticated caller in the tenant, because it is printed on every document they have already been sent.

A tenant that has never set one issues under the deployment-wide Subscription:Invoicing:* configuration, and the response says so with isInheritedFromConfiguration. That fallback exists so upgrading does not blank the seller on every document issued between the deployment and somebody noticing — but it is shared by every tenant on the deployment, which is exactly what a console needs to see.

While Subscription:RequireBillingProfile is on, a tenant with no seller named anywhere blocks a user-initiated charge alongside the subscriber's own missing fields: subscription_billing_profile_incomplete lists merchantLegalName among them. Both halves are required for the same reason — an invoice states a buyer and a seller, and one without the other is not a valid document.

Financial documents

This application issues its own invoices, trial invoices and credit notes, stores them permanently, renders them to PDF, emails them, and serves them back through the API. Stripe remains the payment processor; it is no longer the source of truth for the paperwork.

The change matters because the provider's invoice was the only durable statement of what a subscriber was charged — unreachable when they were down, gone the day we changed processor, and shaped by their template rather than ours.

TypeIssued forNumbered
InvoiceEvery settled positive charge: the initial checkout payment, a card-free trial's conversion charge, a renewal, a paid plan change, a paid quantity increase, and metered overage.INV-{year}-{000001}

Nothing produces a document: a failed, abandoned or still-pending payment attempt, an ordinary cancellation, a quantity decrease scheduled for the period end, or credit being consumed by a later invoice — that appears as a deduction on the invoice it paid for, and issuing a second document for it would count the same value twice.

Append-only. An issued document's money never changes. Corrections are made by issuing a credit note and, where needed, a replacement invoice. The only fields that move are the refund status — a summary of the credit notes, kept so a list can be read without joining — and the PDF and email state.

Numbers may have gaps. A number is allocated before the document is inserted, and one taken by an attempt that then lost a duplicate race is abandoned rather than reused. A gap is a question an auditor can answer from the ledger; a reused number is two documents claiming to be the same one, which nothing can answer.

The PDF is written once. Its SHA-256 is stored beside it, and an issued PDF is never regenerated against a newer template — the file the subscriber already has is the document. Documents are rendered and emailed asynchronously, so a client should read isPdfAvailable rather than assume a download will succeed the instant an invoice appears.

Exactly once, under every replay. Each document is keyed on its source — the payment detail id, the refund id, the subscription and trial instant, the change reference — under a unique index. A redelivered webhook, a retried work item, two workers racing and the recovery sweep all compute the same key, so the second attempt finds the first document rather than allocating another number and sending another email.

Document generation is scheduled through the durable financial-work queue after the money and state transitions commit. A template that throws or a storage bucket that is down can never reverse or duplicate a payment that succeeded.

A document is owed the moment the event happens, not the moment it is scheduled. Every financial event appends an obligation to the subscription in the same write as the transition that caused it, carrying the plan, price, quantities and period as they were then. A change that banks credit appends its obligation inside the very compare-and-set that banks the credit, because it charges nothing and the balance it moves cannot say afterwards which change moved it.

Recovery has no time window. Four passes, and none of them looks back a fixed number of hours — a window would make recovery a function of how long the worker was away, so an outage longer than the window would leave documents that are never issued and nothing that says so. The first reads the recorded obligations, which is the only route by which a banked credit note can be recovered at all. The second and third re-derive from settled payments and confirmed refunds for the case where recording the obligation itself was lost. The fourth walks trials, which leave no payment behind either.

The last three walk forward from a stored mark that is a position rather than a moment: the instant of the last record accounted for and which record it was. Several charges routinely settle in one instant, so an instant alone cannot name a place in a sequence — a page that filled up would either be re-read forever or stepped over. Carrying the identifier makes the order total, so a pass resumes exactly after where the last one stopped.

A late document still describes the charge it was for. The terms come from the obligation rather than from the subscription as it stands, so an invoice written after a plan change still names the plan and the seat count that were actually charged. The figures still come from the payment, which is the only version of them the bank agrees with.

An invoice email is sent at most once. Publishing to the bus and recording that it was published are two writes with nothing joining them, so a crash between them leaves a message that may or may not have gone out. The publish is claimed first by a compare-and-set, so exactly one attempt wins it — and an attempt that finds the claim already taken with no recorded send does not send. Nor does one whose own publish threw: a broker can accept a message and lose the acknowledgement on the way back, so a throw is not evidence of non-delivery. Both record document_mail_outcome_unknown and stop being swept.

A deliberate trade, and the strongest guarantee available: the message envelope carries no identity a broker could deduplicate on, so exactly-once is not reachable and the choice is which way to fail. A subscriber may not receive an email for an invoice they can still see in their history and download; the alternative is two identical invoice emails, which reads as being billed twice and cannot be taken back. A known failure keeps its retry — a render that produced nothing is tried again; only an attempt that may already have sent gives up.

Each mail also carries a MessageId derived from the document id in its data context. That is belt and braces rather than the mechanism, and a mail consumer that deduplicates on it loses nothing by doing so.

POST/api/subscriptions/invoices/{documentId}/resend

Sends a document's mail once more. Platform console only, because whoever calls it is accepting that the subscriber may receive the same invoice twice — the automatic path refuses to take that risk on anybody's behalf. Reopens the delivery and queues it on the same work type every other document uses, so a resend cannot behave differently from a first attempt, and returns as soon as the intent is durable rather than claiming an outcome. 

{
  "success": true,
  "data": {
    "documentId": "b7c1…",
    "documentNumber": "INV-2026-000042",
    "recipient": "billing@northwind.example",
    "messageId": "document-mail:b7c1…",
    "resendGeneration": 1,
    "status": "Queued"
  },
  "error": null
}

recipient is the billing contact snapshotted on the document, not the profile's current one: editing the profile does not redirect an issued document's mail.

resendGeneration counts from one and is part of the identity of the queued work, not a statistic: the work queue admits one item per occurrence and enforces that over finished items too, so each resend has to be its own occurrence or it is refused as a duplicate of the delivery that already ran.

status says what the request did. All three are successes — the mail is going to be sent in every case, which is why saying only that would be useless.

StatusMeaning
QueuedThis request reopened the delivery and queued the send.
JoinedPendingA send was already outstanding and this request joined it. What a double click gets: nothing new was scheduled and no second email will arrive. resendGeneration is the generation that send belongs to, not a new one.
AwaitingSweepThe delivery was reopened but the queue write failed. Not a failure: the delivery sweep finds outstanding documents by their delivery state and needs no queue key, so the send is waiting for that sweep.

Concurrent resends collapse deliberately. Two generations would be two queue items sharing the one document-level mail claim, so the first would send and the second would find the claim taken and send nothing — two accepted requests and one email. Giving each generation its own claim would be worse: a double click could then put two copies of an invoice in somebody's inbox, which is what the claim exists to prevent.

Lifecycle

Seven states. Only three grant anything.

Incomplete grants nothing

Created; the first charge has not confirmed. Expect this immediately after subscribing.

IncompleteExpired grants nothing · terminal

The first charge never completed and the grace period ran out.

Trialing grants

Entitled, inside the trial window.

Active grants

Entitled and paid. The steady state.

PastDue grants

A renewal failed and retries are still running. Deliberately still entitled — a card that expired is not a reason to cut service the same hour.

Unpaid grants nothing

Retries exhausted. Not ended, but no longer entitled.

Canceled grants nothing · terminal

Ended, by request or by non-payment.

Activation is webhook-driven

A subscription becomes Active when the provider's signed webhook confirms the money moved — never when the shopper's browser returns. A redirect can be replayed, forged, bookmarked or lost. Expect a brief Incomplete window after payment and poll; do not treat it as failure.

Branches

Six places the flow forks. Each changes what your application has to handle.

1 · Trial, or no trial

No trial: the subscription goes Incomplete → Active on the first payment. With one: Incomplete → Trialing → Active, and trialEndsAtUtc is set.

2 · Card required, or genuinely free

When there is something to charge, trialRequiresPaymentMethod: true charges the first period at signup and the trial then governs the allowances rather than the price. With false the trial is free and the response carries no checkoutUrl.

When the opening amount is zero — a free plan, a fully discounted first period, a card-free trial — a card can still be required, and collecting one is no longer the same thing as charging for a period. Either requirePaymentMethodUpfront: true on the plan or a trial with trialRequiresPaymentMethod: true is enough:

Nothing is charged, no payment and no invoice are produced by it, and it appears in neither payment nor invoice history — a zero-total period may still produce a zero-value invoice, but that comes from the period, not from the card. 4 · Cancel at period end, or immediately

The default keeps granting until the period already paid for runs out and simply stops renewing: cancelAtPeriodEnd: true, nextPaymentAtUtc: null, status unchanged. Passing immediately=true ends it there and then.

5 · Upgrade, or downgrade

An upgrade is charged straight away for the prorated difference. A downgrade is credited toward future renewals rather than refunded. A trial has paid nothing yet, so its plan simply swaps with no charge or credit either way. Both plans must share a currency; changing interval is allowed.

A plan change starts a full target billing period immediately. Monthly → annual therefore charges the full new annual period at the change instant, less the unused value of the old monthly period and any banked credit. The outgoing usage window is closed and rated under its original allowance and rates before usage continues in the target plan's new window.

6 · Renewal succeeds, or enters dunning

On success the period advances and nextPaymentAtUtc moves on. On decline the subscription goes PastDue, an attempt counter increments, and the next retry is scheduled — the customer keeps access throughout. When retries run out it becomes Unpaid and access stops.

The order to call things, and what to keep from each response.

Buying more, or fewer, units

A quantity change moves the number of units bought, never the plan. If the item carries volume bands, crossing one is an ordinary consequence of the quantity moving — the subscriber keeps their plan, their snapshot and their billing period.

The two directions are not symmetrical, because the money is not. Direction is decided by the item the price is written against — quantityItem on the price — and never by the total across every item. A plan may define items the price does not charge for; moving one of those costs nothing and applies at once.

Decrease At currentPeriodEndUtc. Nothing. The paid period is never refunded, so the units stay available until it ends and the renewal bills the smaller quantity at its band.

One settlement at a time

Every change that moves money — a paid quantity increase, a paid plan change — reserves the subscription before it charges, and the reservation is what the charge is keyed on. While one is settling, normally the length of one card authorization, any other paid change on that subscription is refused: a quantity change with subscription_quantity_change_in_flight, a plan change with the same code. Re-read the subscription and offer it again rather than retrying immediately.

Renewals are the exception, deliberately. A renewal's charge is keyed on the period and the attempt number, neither of which a lost write can move, so a retried renewal already finds the charge it raised instead of raising another — it needs no reservation. What it does do is wait: see below.

Three outcomes, not two. 200 means the units are granted. 409 or 422 means nothing was charged. 502, 503 or 504 with subscription_quantity_charge_unresolved means the provider never answered and the outcome is genuinely unknown — the reservation is kept deliberately, so do not retry into it; show the change as pending and re-read.

Nothing is left unresolved for long. A background sweep picks up any reservation older than Subscription:SettlementReservationGraceMinutes and establishes what the provider actually did — looking for the charge under both the key the attempt reserved and the settlement key an already-paid invoice is recorded under, and, where no record exists, replaying the charge under the reservation's own idempotency key and the routing that attempt used, rather than assuming no money moved. A card removed since is not evidence: only an answer from the provider that the money did not move releases a reservation.

A renewal falling due while a reservation is unresolved is deferred, not billed. The next period would otherwise be charged at the old quantity and the extra units granted on top of it once the reservation settled, paid for only by a proration covering the period that had already closed. The sweep resolves reservations before it renews anything, so a deferral means the outcome is genuinely still unknown; billing resumes on the pass after it clears. One subscriber's unresolved charge never holds up anybody else's billing.

A restricted code and a plan change

A subscription keeps its discount across a plan change — but only where the code covers the target. A code authored for one plan or one price is re-checked against the plan and price being moved to, and a move it does not cover is refused with subscription_discount_not_applicable rather than the discount being quietly carried onto a price it was never offered for, or quietly removed. Show the customer the consequence and let them decide: keep the discount, or give it up by cancelling and subscribing again.

An unrestricted code — which is every code authored before either restriction existed — moves with the subscriber, as does one whose duration or expiry has already ended.

A promotional code and a volume band

Two combinations, answering two questions. The price settles how its automatic discount meets the volume band — see Automatic price discounts — and the plan then settles what a redeemed code adds to that result.

The plan's quantityDiscountCombinationPolicy decides. BestDiscount — the default — applies whichever reduction is larger and does not spend the promotion when the band wins, so a customer's three months of "20% off" are not consumed by periods where the band was bigger. QuantityOnly ignores the code. Stack applies the band and then reduces what is left, which compounds and should be chosen deliberately.

Calendar-aligned yearly prices

A calendar-aligned month prorates the price being charged. A calendar-aligned year cannot: a week of an annual amount is not a quantity anybody can charge. The linked price prices the opening period and nothing else. unitAmountMinor stays the authored annual amount — what a year costs is a commercial decision, and an annual plan is usually not twelve monthly ones.

For a signup on 25 August, the two timings differ only in when the money moves:

At checkoutOn 1 SeptemberCancelling before then
AtBoundary CHF 197.36 (the stub) CHF 10,488.00 charged access ends with the stub; the year is never charged
AtCheckout CHF 10,685.36 (both) the year opens, nothing is charged nothing is refunded; access runs to the end of the year

The stub is 7/31 of the monthly price — calendar dates, counted inclusively, in the subscription’s own time zone. The yearly price’s automatic discount and volume band apply to both the stub and the year; a promotional code applies to the year alone and is consumed once when the year settles.

The linked price is validated at authoring time: same plan, active, one-month cadence, same currency, same quantity item, same tax rate and mode. quoteValidUntilUtc is the earliest instant this quote's proration could no longer hold — the next local midnight in the request's own timeZoneId, since a day fraction is the only figure here that moves with the clock. It is null when nothing is prorated, because then no boundary changes the answer: a flat monthly price quoted today prices the same tomorrow. Re-preview once that instant passes rather than trust a held quote past it.

A condition that would refuse the confirm — an existing subscription, an incomplete billing profile — is reported in blockers rather than as a failure, so a client can show the price and the obstacle together. Each blocker carries the same code a failed POST /api/subscriptions would return, and subscription_billing_profile_incomplete also carries fields, naming which ones are missing. An empty blockers array means confirming would succeed as quoted.

Reading the amounts

Each discount source is reported separately and never as one figure. “CHF 100.00 came off” cannot be turned back into “the yearly price gave 8%, the volume band gave nothing, the coupon gave 2%” — and which of the three it was is exactly what somebody reconciling a two-year-old invoice is asking, by which time the catalogue has moved and the coupon has been retired.

creditAppliedMinor sits below tax because credit pays a bill rather than changing what the bill was for. So netSubtotalMinor + taxAmountMinor - creditAppliedMinor = totalMinor, and grossSubtotalMinor less the three discounts equals netSubtotalMinor.

settlement is populated only on a plan or quantity change, whose amount is a subtraction between two prorated periods rather than a discounted price. It carries an outgoing and a target side — each with its own gross, discounts, tax, full period total and prorated value — plus creditConsumedMinor and netSettlementMinor. A single subtotal cannot explain a mid-period change, and the subscriber asking about one is asking about the two sides.

trial is populated only on a TrialInvoice, and carries the trial window, whether a card was required, and the expected first billing date. originalDocumentId and originalDocumentNumber are populated only on a CreditNote and name the invoice it adjusts.

Rules that bite

Subscribe by code, read by planId

POST /api/subscriptions takes planCode. The portal URL shows you the planId. Sending the id gives subscription_plan_not_found, which reads like the plan is missing when it is not.

Entitlements advise; usage enforces

Reading an entitlement and deciding is a check, not enforcement. Two callers at 149 of 150 both pass it. Only the increment can tell them apart — so for anything counted, gate on POST /api/subscription-usage with enforce: true and act on allowed.

enforce is conditional

enforce only refuses when the meter has overageAllowed: false. On a meter that permits overage it records the excess and returns allowed: true. If you need a hard cap, the plan has to block, not the call.

Enums go out as integers, come back as names

Requests carry "interval": 2; responses carry "interval": "Month". There is no string-enum converter on the request side, so a name fails model binding.

EnumValues
BillingIntervalDay 0 · Week 1 · Month 2 · Year 3
MeterAggregationSum 0 · Max 1 · LastValue 2 (only Sum is supported by the rater)
EntitlementLimitKindBoolean 0 · Count 1 · Unlimited 2

Money is always minor units

300 is EUR 3.00; 8900 is CHF 89.00. The exponent belongs to the currency, and a request carrying both a decimal and a currency invites the two to disagree. JPY has no minor unit; BHD and KWD have three digits.

One open signup or live subscription per organization

Enforced atomically by a unique reservation index covering Incomplete, Trialing, Active and PastDue. The reservation is taken before checkout is created, so concurrent requests cannot both reach payment. A later attempt receives 409 subscription_already_active. Once an abandoned attempt becomes IncompleteExpired, or a subscription ends, the organization may subscribe again.

Periods follow the customer's calendar

timeZoneId decides when a period turns over — "the 1st" means nothing without one. A subscription anchored on the 31st bills on the 28th in February and returns to the 31st in March; the chosen day is never overwritten with a clamped one.

A 404 may mean "not yours"

A plan or subscription belonging to another organization reports as missing rather than forbidden, deliberately — a 403 would confirm the identifier exists somewhere else.
